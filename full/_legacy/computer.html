A New Computer

What does a computer with intelligence feel like?

The Object

Truffle is a personal AI computer. It has no screen and no keyboard, because it is not a machine you operate. It is a machine that works for you. It sits on a desk, draws about as much power as a light bulb, and costs $2,499.

The new thing AI gives a computer is an intent translator. People do not want to use the DoorDash app; they want food. They do not want to use Instagram; they want to be entertained.

Apps are what fractured your life into a thousand companies’ databases. Intelligence is what can put it back together: you say what you want, and the computer works out which apps to drive to get it.

An Intelligent Desktop

The first surface is an intelligent desktop. Connect your apps to a Truffle and it starts reading the context from all of them at once, combining it, and generating interfaces for you, instead of waiting for you to come and prompt it.

An intelligent computer needs somewhere to be proactive, a surface where it can come to you. We call it Home.

Above, the Truffle knew your morning coffee order and placed it, prepared you for your meeting, and handled lunch on its own.

Below, because it holds the context of your whole life, you can ask it to surface something you had forgotten, and it knows which of your apps to use to find it.

It learns from every interaction you have with it, and offers help as new context arrives, before you ask.

A Context Map

The second surface is a context map. The trouble with a million apps is that your life is scattered across a million places on the internet.

Instagram has your likes, Tumblr your inspo, and Cosmos your references, and each one keeps its piece of you behind its own walls.

An intelligent computer can pull all of it in at once, index it, and build a small private internet of your life inside the machine.

Dreaming

The most important thing about an intelligent computer is that it learns over time and takes the shape of its human: a personal model that updates its own weights on the day’s new context, every night, while you are not using it.

The first real moat in AI is a deeply personal model, one that turns your own context into its training data and keeps learning from it.

IBM has estimated that only twenty percent of the world’s data is searchable; the other eighty percent sits on private servers. Personal models on personal devices are what finally reach the other eighty percent, which is the part people actually care about.

Put all of that together and a datacenter does not suffice. An agent that runs all day on your own context, and updates itself every night, is not something you rent by the token.

A system like this has no terminal state. Its demand for tokens does not stop, because the job is never done.

Elon has found out that the datacenter does not work for an always-on agent. Nobody else has bothered to ask yet.

It’ll end up on a computer. A personal AI computer. That’s our bet.
