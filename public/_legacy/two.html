The Three Paths

Cover the planet in datacenters, put them in space, or give every human their own GPU

Just Build More, Bro

The current era of AI was built in datacenters, so the default plan is more of what already works.

The next era needs, at a minimum, a hundred gigawatts of continuous power. Here is how the build toward it is going:17

Delivered capacity has crept from six to eight to maybe nine gigawatts a year while the plans double, and in October even Goldman cut its own 2027 number to seventeen gigawatts against the thirty-six on the schedule. The gap has an explanation: the world of atoms is much harder to build in than the world of bits, and Silicon Valley has spent a decade treating the two as interchangeable. Construction requires concrete, copper, and a place in the utility’s interconnection queue, none of which the technology brothers control. The difference is now visible in warehouses, where GPUs are accumulating faster than anyone can power them. Satya Nadella has said Microsoft’s constraint is not chips but “warm shells” to plug them into. When 12,000 H100s meant for Tesla were redirected to X, Musk explained that they “would have just sat in the warehouse,” and his CFO had already told investors the same thing on an earnings call: you can’t just order GPUs and turn them on; you need a datacenter first.18

Few people in the industry say this directly, because the stakes of being wrong are so large. If “build more” fails, a meaningful part of the AI trade unwinds, and a share of U.S. GDP growth goes with it. So the plan keeps being restated rather than re-examined.

Put Them Into Space

The second candidate solves the siting problem by leaving the planet. In orbit the sun never sets, land costs nothing, and there are no neighbors to object. Serious teams are working on it: Google’s Project Suncatcher plans two prototype satellites carrying TPUs by early 2027,19 and Starcloud, now valued at $2.3B, flew an Nvidia H100 to orbit in November 2025 and trained a small model on the works of Shakespeare.20

The physics is where it gets difficult. In vacuum there is no air and no water, so every joule a chip consumes must leave as infrared radiation. At datacenter scale that means roughly 500,000 square meters of radiators per 100 MW: thousands of tons of panel, delivered by heavy-lift launches that carry no chips at all. Radiators and solar arrays come to about two thirds of a satellite’s mass, and nothing in orbit can be repaired. Google’s own analysis puts the launch cost needed to make the economics work below $200 per kilogram; today’s price is about fourteen times that.21

In practice that means Starship has to work, and work at scale, and launch prices have to keep falling at their historical rate for most of another decade. Google’s own analysis puts the crossing in the mid-2030s. None of that helps in 2027, however hard you believe in Elon.

“Plurality must never be posited without necessity.”William of Ockham, c. 1320

A GPU Per Person

Ockham’s rule is about explanations, but it holds for plans too: do not add moving parts you do not need. The first candidate has to cover the country in buildings that everyone hates. The second has to solve daily flights of the largest, heaviest rocket ever built. The third is to sell people a GPU with the models already loaded. It is the simplest way to put AI in front of a billion people, and it is the one the industry has ignored.

It works because it stops fighting the constraint. Spread across a billion homes instead of a few hundred giant buildings, the hundred gigawatts comes to about a hundred watts each, roughly what a bright light bulb draws, carried by a grid that was built decades ago for exactly that kind of load. Nothing at that scale needs a permit, a transmission line, a cooling tower, or a county meeting.

A cloud build needs trillions of dollars raised before the first token is served, and repaid on a guess about future utilization. A personal machine is paid for up front by the person who uses it. Supply arrives with demand instead of years ahead of it, and none of it depends on a rate cycle.

AI will diffuse through the economy as a GPU per person.

I know, I know: it’s extremely unsexy compared to mass drivers on the Moon, Starship being caught by chopsticks, or nuclear-powered datacenters covering the Sahara.

But it’s exactly for those reasons that it becomes the most likely path.
